Yes, AAdvantage miles can be transferred to another person — American sells
the service directly. And in over a decade of moving miles around, the paid
account-to-account transfer is almost never the move we'd recommend. Not
because it doesn't work; because the fee structure quietly makes it one of
the most expensive ways to solve the problem it exists for.

Here are the three ways to get American miles where they need to be, ranked
the way we'd rank them for our own accounts.

## 1. Don't move the miles — book the ticket

The fact most people asking this question are missing: **you can book an award
ticket for anyone, directly from your own account, for free.** The passenger
name on the booking doesn't have to be the account holder. Spouse, parent,
friend, client — you book, they fly, no miles change accounts and no fee is
paid.

If the goal is "my miles, their seat", this is the whole answer, and it makes
the paid transfer below irrelevant nine times out of ten. The only thing a
transfer achieves that this doesn't is putting the miles under the other
person's control — which matters, but far less often than people assume.

## 2. The official paid transfer — read the fee first

American's account-to-account transfer runs through its buy-and-transfer
portal, priced as a per-mile charge plus a processing fee. The exact schedule
shifts, so check it at the moment of purchase — but the shape of it doesn't
shift: **on small transfers, the fees can approach what the miles themselves
are worth.** Moving a few thousand miles to top up someone's balance is the
worst case; the flat processing fee is spread across almost nothing.

When does it make sense? When control genuinely matters — consolidating a
lapsing account into an active one, or an estate situation — and the amount
is large enough to dilute the flat fee. Run the per-mile total including every
fee before committing, and compare it against the alternatives on either side
of this list. It loses that comparison more often than not.

## 3. Top up the destination account directly

If the real problem is that the *other* account is short — the family member
whose balance is 30,000 shy of the redemption — then moving your miles across
is solving it from the expensive side. Miles can be delivered straight into
the account that needs them, priced per mile, with no transfer fee layered on
top. That's the trade we quote every day: the current AAdvantage rate is on
our [American Airlines page](/buy/american-aadvantage), and the quote puts
the full cost next to your shortfall before you decide anything.

The comparison worth running is always the same three-way: cost of booking
from your own account (free), cost of the official transfer (fees included),
cost of topping up directly. Whichever is cheapest for *your* amount wins —
and the answer changes with the size of the shortfall, which is exactly why
we'd never give you a one-line rule.

## The one thing not to do

Don't "transfer" miles by sharing account logins and booking from inside
someone else's account without the programme's blessing. AAdvantage's terms
are blunt about unauthorised account access, and a frozen account with a
six-figure balance is a far worse outcome than any transfer fee. Every route
above — booking for others, the official transfer, a direct top-up — exists
precisely so nobody has to do that.
