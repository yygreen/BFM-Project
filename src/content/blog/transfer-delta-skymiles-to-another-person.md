Yes, Delta lets you move SkyMiles into another member's account. The price has
two parts, and the second part is the one that decides whether this is a good
idea: **1 cent per mile, plus a flat $30 processing fee per transaction.**

The flat fee is why the same transfer can be cheap or absurd depending only on
its size.

| You move | You pay | Per mile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1,000 miles | $40 | 4.0¢ |
| 5,000 miles | $80 | 1.6¢ |
| 10,000 miles | $130 | 1.3¢ |
| 30,000 miles | $330 | 1.1¢ |

Minimum 1,000 miles, maximum 30,000 per transaction. You can send up to 150,000
miles a year and receive up to 300,000.

The shape of that table is the whole article. **Topping up a friend's account by
a few thousand miles is one of the most expensive things you can do with
SkyMiles.** At the bottom of the range you are paying four times what the miles
are plausibly worth. At the top the fee dilutes to almost nothing and the
transfer is priced sensibly.

## The free thing to do instead

**You can book a Delta award ticket for anyone, from your own account, for
nothing.** The passenger name does not have to match the account holder.
Spouse, parent, friend, colleague: you book, they fly, no miles change hands and
no fee is paid.

If the goal is "my miles, their seat", that is the entire answer and the fee
table above is irrelevant. The transfer buys you exactly one thing the free
route does not, which is putting the miles under someone else's control so they
can choose the flight themselves. Worth paying for occasionally. Worth checking
first, always.

Delta has no free pooling feature. United does, which is the one place these two
programs differ meaningfully on this question.

## The cost nobody puts in the table

Move 30,000 miles and the $330 is only the visible half. You also no longer have
30,000 SkyMiles. Replacing them means buying them, and that replacement price is
the honest way to value what left your account.

So a 30,000-mile transfer is not a $330 decision. It is $330 plus 30,000 miles
of your own future travel. Almost every guide to this question quotes the fee
and stops there, which makes transferring look cheaper than it is whenever both
parties would have used the miles anyway.

## When their account is short

The case the fee table cannot solve: **their account is short and yours is not
overflowing.** They need 70,000 miles, you hold 40,000, and handing yours over
produces two accounts that both fall short.

## The one thing not to do

Do not move miles by sharing account credentials and booking from inside someone
else's account. The SkyMiles rules are blunt about unauthorised access, and a
frozen account holding a six-figure balance costs far more than any fee above.
Every legitimate route exists so nobody has to take that risk.
