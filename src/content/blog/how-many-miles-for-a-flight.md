The honest answer is that for six of the twenty-two programs on this site,
there is no number. Delta, United, Southwest, JetBlue, Frontier and Flying Blue
price an award off the cash fare on the day, which means the miles needed for
your flight are whatever the search says this afternoon and possibly something
else tomorrow.

For the other sixteen there is a real number, and you can look it up before
you spend anything. Which group you are in decides how you should shop.

## Three ways a program decides the price

**A fixed chart.** The program publishes a table: this region to that region,
in this cabin, costs this many miles. ANA, EVA Air, KrisFlyer, Turkish and
LifeMiles still work this way. The number is knowable in advance, it is the
same in January and in July unless the program changes the chart, and it is why
these programs are the ones people plan around.

**Distance bands.** The price follows how far you actually fly, in steps. Both
Avios programs, Asia Miles, Atmos Rewards and Etihad's Saver awards work like
this. Still knowable in advance, and the arithmetic rewards short hops and
punishes connections through the wrong hub.

**Dynamic pricing.** The miles are pegged to what the ticket costs in cash, so
a peak-season seat costs multiples of the same seat in November. There is
nothing to look up. The airline's own search is the only source of truth, and
it is only true for that search.

Plenty of programs run two systems at once. Lufthansa, Air Canada and American
price their own flights dynamically and price partner airlines off a published
chart, which is exactly why the partner award is so often the better buy.

## Why we will not print a table of miles per route

Because it would be wrong within months, and a wrong number here costs someone
a purchase. Three examples from the last two years, all of them programs on
this page:

- **Lufthansa** moved Lufthansa Group flights to dynamic pricing on 3 June
  2025 and devalued the remaining partner chart on the same day. Every guide
  quoting a Lufthansa business class number from before that date is describing
  a system that no longer exists.
- **Turkish** raised award prices in February 2024, then raised partner pricing
  again in December 2025 without advance notice.
- **Emirates** raised the miles needed for First, Business and Premium Economy
  rewards by around 15% from 20 May 2026.

Anyone publishing "45,000 miles to Europe" as a standing fact is publishing a
snapshot. The mechanism is durable. The number is not.

## The four things that move the number more than the route does

**One way against return.** Chart programs are not consistent about which they
quote, and ANA prices international awards as a round trip that has to be
booked as one. Reading a return price as a one way is the single most common
way people underbuy.

**Whose aircraft you fly.** In Miles & More, Aeroplan and AAdvantage the same
journey has two prices, one for the airline's own metal and one for a partner.
The gap is often large enough to decide the whole trip.

**Whether a saver seat exists.** A chart price is a price for a seat the
airline has released at that level. If none is released on your date, the
number you looked up is not available to you at any point.

**Taxes and carrier surcharges.** These are paid in cash on top of the miles,
and on British Airways and Lufthansa in particular they can run into the
hundreds. Miles cover the fare. They do not cover the bill.

## How to get your own number, in the right order

1. **Search the actual flight in that program's own award search**, on your
   real dates, for your real party size. Not a guide, not a forum post from
   last year.
2. **Write down what the search says**, including whether it is one way or
   return and whether it is per person.
3. **Subtract what you already hold.** The gap is what you need, and it is
   usually smaller than people assume.
4. **Add a margin.** Ten to fifteen percent covers a date change or a modest
   move in a dynamic price between the quote and the booking. Buying twice
   costs more than buying slightly long once.
5. **Test it against the cash fare** before you commit. Our
   [calculators](/calculator) price a mileage amount against a real ticket for
   each program and will tell you when paying cash is the better trade. They
   say so often enough that we trust them.

## Where the miles come from

Once you know the number, the question is what it costs to get there. Buying
directly from an airline is priced for the impulse top-up and is usually the
most expensive route to a balance, which is why the gap between an airline's
own buy-miles page and a trader rate is normally worth checking rather than
assuming.

Live per-mile rates and delivery windows for all twenty-two programs are on
