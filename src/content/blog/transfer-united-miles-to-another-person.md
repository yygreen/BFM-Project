United will move MileagePlus miles into another member's account for **$7.50 per
500 miles, plus a $30 processing fee**, up to 100,000 miles a year. That works
out at 1.5 cents a mile before the fixed fee, which makes it the most expensive
account-to-account transfer among the US majors. Delta charges 1 cent a mile,
Southwest charges 1 cent and skips the processing fee entirely.

Before you price that up, read the next section, because on United it usually
makes the question go away.

## Pool the miles instead. It is free

United runs **MileagePlus Pooling**, and it costs nothing. Any member over 18
can start a pool and invite up to four others, five people in total. Family,
friends and travel companions all qualify, children included. Miles contributed
to the pool can be redeemed by the pool for award travel, and as of 2026 pooled
miles work on United's partner airlines too, which was the change that made the
feature genuinely useful rather than merely convenient.

United was the first of the big four US programs to offer this. Delta, American
and Southwest have nothing equivalent. So the single most useful fact about
transferring United miles is that on United, most people should not.

A pool covers the common case completely: a household with miles spread across
several accounts and one trip to book. No fee, no cap of the kind the paid
transfer carries, and nobody has to hand their balance to anyone.

## The other free route: just book their ticket

**You can book a United award ticket for anyone from your own account at no
cost.** The passenger does not have to be the account holder. If what you want
is "my miles, their seat", this has always been the answer and it remains the
answer.

Between pooling and booking for others, the paid transfer is left with a narrow
job: moving miles permanently into someone else's control, when that person is
not someone you would put in a five-person pool.

## What the paid transfer costs when you do use it

The $30 sits on top of every transaction regardless of size, so small transfers
are hit hardest.

| You move | You pay | Per mile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2,000 miles | $60 | 3.0¢ |
| 10,000 miles | $180 | 1.8¢ |
| 30,000 miles | $480 | 1.6¢ |
| 100,000 miles | $1,530 | 1.5¢ |

Even at the top of the range it never gets cheap, because the per-mile rate
itself is the expensive part. That is the structural difference from Delta,
where a large transfer eventually becomes reasonable.

And as with any transfer, the fee is only the visible half of the cost. Move
30,000 miles and you are also 30,000 miles poorer; replacing them means buying
them, which is the honest way to value what left your account. A 30,000-mile
transfer is a $480 decision plus 30,000 miles of your own travel.

## When buying into their account is the better trade

Pooling solves the household case. It does not solve this one: **the account
that needs the miles is short, and yours is not overflowing.** They need 90,000
for a business-class booking, you hold 50,000, and moving yours across leaves
two accounts that both fall short.

Buying miles directly into the account making the booking fixes that from the
right side. Your balance stays where it is, the miles arrive where they will be
spent, and there is no transfer fee stacked on a per-mile price.

Two things worth saying plainly:

- **Our minimum order on MileagePlus is 80,000 miles**, the highest on our

  official transfer is the right one.

## The one thing not to do

Do not move miles by sharing logins and booking from inside someone else's
account. MileagePlus terms are explicit about unauthorised access, and a frozen
account with a large balance costs far more than any fee here. Pooling exists
precisely so that families never need to.
