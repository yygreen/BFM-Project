Yes, Southwest lets you move Rapid Rewards points into someone else's account.
It costs **1 cent per point**, sold in 500-point blocks at $5 each, and you can
move up to **60,000 points a year**. The points show up in the other account
within about 72 hours.

Southwest is the gentlest of the big US programs here, because there is no flat
processing fee bolted on top. Delta and United both charge $30 a transaction
before the first point moves, which makes their small transfers punishing.
Southwest's price is the same whether you move 500 points or 50,000.

That still does not make transferring the right move most of the time.

## The free thing to do instead

**You can book a Southwest flight for anyone, with your own points, at no
cost.** The traveller does not need to be you, and they do not need to be in
your account. You enter their name as the passenger, pay with your points, and
the booking is theirs.

If what you actually want is "my points, their seat", you are done. No transfer,
no fee, no 60,000 cap. This is the answer for the large majority of people who
search for how to transfer Southwest points, and it is worth checking against
your own situation before you spend anything.

The transfer only does one thing the free route does not: it puts the points
under the other person's control, so they can book what they like when they
like. That is a real reason. It is just a narrower one than it first appears.

## What a transfer actually costs you

The fee is the small half of the bill. Here is the whole one.

Moving 20,000 points costs **$200**. It also costs you 20,000 points, which you
no longer have. To put those points back in your account you would have to buy
them, and that is the honest way to price what you gave up.

So the real cost of a transfer is the fee **plus the replacement cost of the
points**. Nearly every guide to this question quotes the first number and stops.
A 20,000-point transfer is not a $200 decision. It is a $200 decision plus
20,000 points of your own travel.

That matters most when both people are going to fly anyway. If your points were
going to be spent on your own trip, transferring them does not save the
household anything, it just moves which of you pays cash later.

## When buying into their account is the better trade

There is one situation where none of the above helps: **the destination account
is short, and yours is not overflowing.** The other person needs 60,000 points
for a booking, you have 25,000, and giving away yours leaves two accounts that
each cannot book anything.

Buying points straight into the account that needs them solves it from the
right side. Nothing leaves your balance, the points land where the booking will
be made, and there is no transfer fee layered on top of a per-point price.

## The one thing not to do

Do not solve this by handing over your account login. Rapid Rewards terms are
clear about unauthorised account access, and a frozen account with a large
balance in it is a far worse outcome than any fee discussed above. Every route
here exists so that nobody has to do that.
